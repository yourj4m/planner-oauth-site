A minimal weekly planner

fweek shows your week on one page: what to do and what is on, day by day, with lists for everything that has no day yet. It runs on Windows, Linux and Android, and it is free, open-source software.

Download Source code

What it does

  • A week view with a line per task or event, and a month view.
  • Lists for tasks without a day, which can be planned onto a day by dragging.
  • Reminders, repeating tasks, notes, places and colours.
  • Events from calendars you subscribe to (for example Google Calendar's iCal address), shown in your week.
  • Backups and exports to files, and daily copies kept on your device.

Your planner, on your devices

fweek keeps your planner on your device. There is no fweek account and no fweek server; the developer never receives your planner.

To have the same planner on your computer and your phone, and to get it back on a new device, fweek can sync through a place you choose:

  • Google Drive. In Settings → Sync, choose “Connect Google account”, pick your account on Google’s own sign-in page and allow access. fweek then keeps its files in a hidden folder of its own in your Google Drive and saves every change there shortly after you make it. On another device, connecting the same account brings your planner back. fweek cannot see or change any other file in your Drive.
  • Nextcloud. Your own Nextcloud server, with an app password.

What fweek asks Google for

Your Google account’s email address, to show which account is connected, and access to the app’s own hidden data folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata), to keep your planner there. Nothing else.

Read the privacy policy for what is stored, where, and how to remove it.
